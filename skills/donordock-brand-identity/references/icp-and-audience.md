# DonorDock Ideal Customer Profile & Audience Guide

This reference defines who DonorDock is built for, who it isn't for, and how to talk to each audience segment.

---

## The One-Sentence ICP

**We win with community-funded nonprofits that are outgrowing duct-tape systems and want fundraising to feel lighter, not louder.**

---

## Firmographic ICP (Data-Backed)

Based on analysis of DonorDock's best-performing customers:

| Dimension | Sweet Spot | Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual revenue** | $1M–$10M | $500K–$34M (center of gravity: low single-digit millions) |
| **Staff size** | ~39 employees | 15–75 |
| **CRM users** | 11 (median) | 7–15 |
| **Contact records** | ~7,000 (median) | 5K–50K |
| **Gift records** | ~17,000 (median) | Data-dense organizations |
| **Annual investment** | $5K+/year | Willing to invest in reducing friction |

### Key Insight
DonorDock's best customers are **operationally complex but not enterprise-scale**. Even when revenue goes to $34M, user counts stay in the 7-14 range and staff stays under 50. This means:

**"Operationally complex nonprofits that cannot afford enterprise CRM overhead."**

That's the positioning wedge.

## Behavioral ICP

### Fundraising Model
- Revenue driven by a **broad base of individual supporters**, not a handful of major donors
- Natural community flywheel: supporters, volunteers, members, patrons, parents
- Stewardship is a **365-day practice**, not just event- or campaign-based

### Team & Ownership
- Fundraising owned by a **lean team (3+ FTE)** — not a solo operator, a one-person development shop, or a board-only effort
- Clear accountability for donor follow-up and relationship health
- Growth has introduced complexity they can no longer "remember their way through"

### Operational Reality
- Running fundraising across **disparate systems** (CRM, spreadsheets, email tools, online giving, notes)
- Important work falls through cracks due to manual processes and context switching
- Want fewer systems doing more — not more tools doing less

### Buying Readiness
- Willing and able to invest $5K+/year to reduce friction and increase focus
- Revenue alone isn't the qualifier — **readiness** is
- Value outcomes (follow-up, clarity, time saved), not just features

### Mindset
- Want software that *actively helps them steward relationships*
- Care about doing fundraising *well*, not just doing more of it
- Open to change if it clearly makes their work feel more human and manageable

## Who This ISN'T For (Disqualifiers)

Just as valuable as knowing who we serve is knowing who we don't. This helps everyone qualify faster and say "no" with confidence.

| Disqualifier | Why |
|---|---|
| Nonprofits under $1M revenue | Below ICP revenue floor; won't feel the pain DonorDock solves; may not justify the investment |
| Nonprofits with under 3 FTE | Don't have the team structure our platform is built for; solo operators and 2-person shops better served by lighter tools |
| Founder-led nonprofits with no fundraising team | Need a simpler tool or aren't ready for CRM yet |
| Grant-only or government-funded organizations | Individual donor management isn't their primary need |
| Organizations with <2 CRM users | Multi-user collaboration pain hasn't kicked in yet |
| Enterprise nonprofits ($50M+) needing deep customization | Better served by Blackbaud, Salesforce NPSP, or Virtuous |
| Organizations that primarily need peer-to-peer fundraising | Givebutter or Classy might be better starting points |
| Individual congregational churches (weekly offering / tithing use case) | Planning Center, Breeze, Tithe.ly own this use case. Faith-based 501(c)(3) nonprofits WITH development programs (parachurch orgs, religious social services, faith-based foundations) ARE a fit — the distinction is congregational-giving vs donor-development fundraising |

## Buyer & Urgency Signals

When these show up, a prospect is likely a great fit:

- Has a **dedicated fundraising lead** (not just the founder)
- Board is asking for better reporting and data
- Multi-user CRM friction (permissions, consistency, trust in data)
- Pressure to "get out of the weeds" operationally
- Recently experienced a missed follow-up, lost donor, or embarrassing data error
- Current tool contract is expiring or up for renewal
- Just hired a new development director who wants better systems

### Sales-Ready Messaging for These Signals
- "Confidence in your donor data"
- "Fewer spreadsheets, fewer workarounds"
- "Clarity for fundraisers *and* leadership"
- "A system your whole team will actually use"

## How We Talk About Our Customers

### Language to Use
- **Nonprofit leaders** and **founders** (not "clients" or "users")
- They **wear many hats** (fundraiser, event planner, volunteer coordinator, accountant)
- They have **tool overload** (too many disconnected systems)
- They are **doing good work** but are **drowning** in not enough time, not enough resources
- They are **dedicated to their mission** above all else
- They are **past spreadsheet survivability** — their data is too complex for manual tracking

### Language to Avoid
- Don't call them "small nonprofits" — use "growing nonprofits" or "lean teams"
- Don't imply they're behind or doing things wrong
- Don't treat them as unsophisticated — they're smart people with too much on their plates
- Don't assume they know CRM jargon — explain terms when introducing them

---

## How We Talk About the Nonprofit Sector

### Our Stance
- This sector is **underserved** by technology — tools are often built for enterprises then awkwardly scaled down
- Nonprofits provide **immense value** to their communities
- Nonprofits need to **operate like for-profit businesses** — with rigor, systems, and strategy
- They aren't asking for handouts — they're asking for **support and partnership** to deliver value
- There isn't enough time and there aren't enough resources, but that makes **focus** even more critical

### How This Shows Up in Content
- Validate the sector: "The work nonprofits do matters enormously."
- Acknowledge the struggle: "Limited resources are real. Burnout is real."
- Elevate the aspiration: "You deserve tools that match your ambition."
- Never pity: "You don't need sympathy — you need systems that work."

## How We Talk About Our Team

- We are a **lean team**, just like those we serve
- We reference our **values** often (transparency, simplicity, versatility, empathy, confidence)
- We are **open and transparent** in how we communicate
- We are **fun** — we don't take ourselves too seriously
- We are **dedicated to serving and providing value first** — to the sector and to nonprofit leaders
- Our team members are real people, accessible and available

## Audience-Specific Tone Adjustments

### New Prospects (Top of Funnel)
- **Tone:** Excited, Instructive, Personable
- **Priority:** Build trust fast. Lead with empathy for their pain, then show the possibility.
- **CTA style:** Low-pressure invitations. "See DonorDock in action." "Book a free demo."
- **Proof points:** G2 ratings, user count, "no contracts" reassurance.

### Active Evaluators (Mid-Funnel)
- **Tone:** Confident, Informative, Comparison-ready
- **Priority:** Help them see how DonorDock fits their specific needs.
- **CTA style:** Demo-focused. "Let us show you how this works for organizations like yours."
- **Proof points:** Feature comparisons, specific customer stories, implementation speed.

### Existing Customers
- **Tone:** Informative, Straightforward, Personable
- **Priority:** Help them get more value from the platform. Celebrate their wins.
- **CTA style:** Feature adoption. "Have you tried Smart Nudges yet?"
- **Proof points:** Product updates, tips and tricks, community success stories.

### Responding to Complaints
- **Tone:** Calm, Empathetic, Helpful & Informative
- **Priority:** Acknowledge the frustration, take ownership, offer a path forward.
- **Never:** Be defensive, dismissive, or blame the user.

### Public / Sector Audience (Non-Customers)
- **Tone:** Confident, Upbeat, All-inclusive, Encouraging
- **Priority:** Position DonorDock as a thought leader and ally to the whole nonprofit sector.
- **CTA style:** Content-forward. "Read the full guide." "Listen to the podcast."
- **Proof points:** Original research, expert interviews, sector data.
