## ICP segments (post-upmarket move)

### Primary: Growing Nonprofit Development Director
- **Persona:** Development Director at a nonprofit with $1M-$5M annual revenue, 1,000-10,000 contacts, small-to-mid development team (2-5 staff)
- **Typical title:** Director of Development, VP of Advancement, Chief Development Officer
- **Operating reality:** Managing multiple fundraising programs (annual fund, major gifts, events, monthly giving), wearing some-but-not-all hats, reports to ED/CEO
- **Pain:** Growing out of Bloomerang/DonorPerfect contact-tier pricing; or graduating from spreadsheets; needs modern AI-era tools but can't afford enterprise Virtuous/Salesforce
- **Buying authority:** Evaluates and recommends; ED/board approves budget
- **Success metric:** Donor retention, YoY revenue growth, staff hours saved

### Primary: Growing Nonprofit Executive Director (upper end)
- **Persona:** ED of $2M-$8M nonprofit with 5-15 staff including a small development team
- **Typical title:** Executive Director, President & CEO
- **Operating reality:** Sets fundraising strategy, personally cultivates major donors, oversees systems/technology decisions
- **Pain:** Tool sprawl (CRM + email + text + website + giving platform); inconsistent donor experience; data silos
- **Buying authority:** Final decision; board informs
- **Success metric:** Revenue growth, donor retention, organizational efficiency

### Secondary: Mid-sized Nonprofit Development Team Member
- **Persona:** Development Associate, Database Manager, Data Coordinator at $5M-$15M nonprofit
- **Operating reality:** Day-to-day CRM user, data entry + reporting + campaign execution
- **Pain:** Inherited clunky system; wants modern UX + AI to reduce manual work
- **Buying authority:** Recommends to DOD/VP; not primary decider
- **Success metric:** Hours saved, data quality, campaign execution speed

### Secondary: Nonprofit Consultant / Fractional Fundraiser
- **Persona:** Independent fundraising consultant serving 3-10 growing nonprofits
- **Operating reality:** Recommends CRM + tools to clients; often implements; drives switcher activity
- **Pain:** Needs a tool they can recommend confidently across clients
- **Buying authority:** Influences, doesn't buy directly
- **Success metric:** Client satisfaction, implementation ease

### NOT in ICP (per brand-positioning.md)
- Small nonprofits under $1M revenue / under 1,000 contacts (LGL territory)
- Volunteer-run organizations
- Solo EDs with no development team
- Churches (congregational giving use case — Planning Center territory)
- Enterprise / $20M+ organizations (Virtuous/Blackbaud territory)
- International / non-US nonprofits

## Buyer journey stages

### Stage 1: Unaware
Doesn't know they have a problem. Using spreadsheets or inherited legacy system.
**Search behavior:** "how to track donations," "how to organize donor data," "best spreadsheet for fundraising"
**Content fit:** Fundraising Strategy pillar content; podcast episodes; bottom-of-funnel ads

### Stage 2: Problem-aware
Recognizes spreadsheet/legacy tool is limiting growth. Hasn't started vendor research.
**Search behavior:** "nonprofit fundraising software," "how to track donor communications," "donor retention rate 2026," "why do donors stop giving"
**Content fit:** Donor Retention + Fundraising Strategy pillars; category education AEO Qs; gated "When to upgrade" asset

### Stage 3: Solution-aware
Researching the category. Knows nonprofit CRM exists. Starting to compare approaches (CRM vs database vs all-in-one).
**Search behavior:** "best nonprofit CRM," "nonprofit CRM vs donor database," "nonprofit CRM with AI," "nonprofit CRM with unlimited contacts"
**Content fit:** Nonprofit CRM pillar content; "how to choose" articles; buyer's guide; Smart Stewardship positioning articles

### Stage 4: Product-aware
Evaluating specific vendors. Reading comparison pages. Attending demos.
**Search behavior:** "DonorDock vs Bloomerang," "DonorPerfect alternative," "Givebutter vs DonorDock pricing," "how much does Bloomerang cost," "Neon CRM review"
**Content fit:** All 10 /compare/* pages; /pricing; demo / trial CTAs; customer success stories

### Stage 5: Most-aware
Ready to buy. Final-stage validation, objection handling, internal sell-through.
**Search behavior:** "DonorDock pricing," "DonorDock reviews," "convince your team DonorDock," "DonorDock migration timeline"
**Content fit:** /pricing, /convince-your-team, /reviews (to build), customer success, demo CTAs, premium onboarding details

## Content tone guidance by segment

- **Development Director:** Operational, practical, "how to run this better." Smart Stewardship as methodology.
- **Executive Director:** Strategic, thought-leadership, "how this affects your mission." Smart Stewardship as philosophy.
- **Development Team:** Tactical, screenshot-heavy, "here's exactly how to do X." Otto AI and Action Board examples.
- **Consultant:** Framework-centric, repeatable systems, "what I recommend to clients."

Every piece of content should name the primary segment it targets (in internal notes or briefs) so tone and examples match.
