# Competitor SEO Audit: Network for Good (Bonterra)

**Audit date:** 2026-04-22
**Baseline:** seo-brain 2026-04 baseline
**Competitor:** Network for Good (now Bonterra Network for Good), acquired by Bonterra Jan 2022
**Primary URL (historical):** https://www.networkforgood.com
**Primary URL (current):** https://www.bonterratech.com/product/network-for-good
**Migration status:** Active 301 redirects from networkforgood.com to bonterratech.com (confirmed on root and interior URLs)
**DonorDock baseline:** https://www.donordock.com | $500/mo, 1% platform fee, unlimited contacts

---

## 1. Executive Summary

- **NFG no longer exists as a standalone SEO property.** The entire networkforgood.com domain 301-redirects to bonterratech.com. NFG is now a sub-brand ("Bonterra Network for Good") with a single product page (`/product/network-for-good`) and inherited blog equity folded into the Bonterra content hub. The redirect strategy consolidates backlink authority but forfeits the brand-specific topical authority NFG had built over 20+ years.
- **Bonterra has a large but diffuse content footprint.** The sitemap index exposes ~1,550+ URLs across posts (993), case studies (252), training (185), pages (78), case-studies (185), and post-sitemap2 (36). This dwarfs DonorDock but is spread across 10 product lines — NFG-specific depth is shallow.
- **Bonterra targets high-volume category keywords (not small-nonprofit intent) and omits DonorDock from most lists.** The flagship "31+ top nonprofit software solutions for 2026" (Apr 2026) names Bloomerang, DonorPerfect, Little Green Light, Givebutter, Keela, and 26 others — but not DonorDock. Only the older "nonprofit-crm-guide" (Jan 2025) mentions DonorDock, describing it dismissively as "entry-level" with "limited communication features."
- **AEO/GEO posture is weak-to-moderate.** Bonterra has FAQ schema on the NFG product page (good), Article + Organization + BreadcrumbList schema on blog posts, but **no visible author bylines** on major guides (major gifts, year-end) — a clear E-E-A-T gap. There is **no llms.txt** (404) and **no AI-bot rules** in robots.txt — Bonterra is passively indexable by AI crawlers but is not proactively optimized for GEO citation. DonorDock, by contrast, has an llms.txt and explicit AI-bot rules (though it *blocks* training crawlers, which is a trade-off).
- **Three clear openings for DonorDock:** (1) Price-transparency and contract-flexibility content (NFG has 1-yr non-cancellable contracts, hidden pricing, and 4.75% transaction fees per review data); (2) "NFG alternatives" / "leaving Bonterra" migration-intent content (NFG's G2 reviews cite data-loss and support-failure horror stories); (3) Author-bylined, small-nonprofit-specific E-E-A-T content (Bonterra's is byline-free and enterprise-leaning).

## 4. Technical SEO Posture

### robots.txt (verbatim, bonterratech.com/robots.txt)
```
Crawl-delay: 10
# START YOAST BLOCK
# ---------------------------
User-agent: *
Disallow:

Sitemap: https://www.bonterratech.com/sitemap_index.xml
# ---------------------------
# END YOAST BLOCK
```
- **AI crawler posture: permissive.** No rules for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot, CCBot, Bytespider, Applebot-Extended, or meta-externalagent. All fall under the open wildcard. This means Bonterra content is eligible to be used for AI training and cited in AI answers.
- **Crawl-delay of 10 seconds** is generous — may throttle discovery on a 1,600+ URL site.
- **Yoast-generated** (WordPress origin confirmed). Single sitemap index declared.

### Sitemap structure
- Sitemap index at `/sitemap_index.xml`
- 11 child sitemaps organized by content type (posts, pages, case studies, products, training, etc.)
- Clean hierarchical structure suggests XML validity; lastmod timestamps not inspected but typical for Yoast.

### Schema markup validity
**NFG product page (`/product/network-for-good`):**
- `WebPage`, `WebSite`, `Organization`, `BreadcrumbList` (valid baseline)
- `FAQPage` with 4 Question/Answer entries (valid — strong AEO signal)
- `ReadAction`, `SearchAction`, `EntryPoint` (navigation/search enrichment)
- `ImageObject` present
- **Missing:** `Product`, `SoftwareApplication`, `Offer`, `AggregateRating`, `Review`. This is a significant miss — NFG is a product, yet the page is schema-marked as generic WebPage, not `SoftwareApplication` with pricing/rating enrichment. DonorDock should ensure its product pages use `SoftwareApplication` + `AggregateRating` + `Offer` to outrank.

**Blog posts (e.g., donor-management-software):**
- `Article`, `Person` (author reference in schema), `Organization`, `BreadcrumbList`, `ImageObject` — all present
- **However: the rendered page does not expose an author byline visibly.** This is a mismatch between schema and visible UX — Google rewards visible author info for E-E-A-T.

**Blog posts commonly missing FAQ schema** (major-gifts article confirmed none), despite articles including Q&A-style sections. This is an easy-win gap Bonterra hasn't closed.

### Technical summary
| Signal | NFG/Bonterra | DonorDock (baseline) | Advantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI bot access | Permissive (no explicit rules) | Blocks GPTBot/ClaudeBot/Google-Extended/CCBot/Applebot-Extended/meta-externalagent | Bonterra (for citation); DonorDock (for IP protection) |
| Sitemap | Clean, indexed, 11 child sitemaps | `sitemap.xml` present | Even |
| Schema — product page | `FAQPage`, `WebPage`, `Org`, `Breadcrumb` — no `SoftwareApplication`/`Offer` | TBD (audit DonorDock separately) | **Opportunity for DonorDock** |
| Schema — blog | `Article` + `Person` + `Breadcrumb` but no visible byline | TBD | **Opportunity for DonorDock** |
| Crawl-delay | 10 sec (slow) | Likely none | DonorDock |
| Platform | WordPress + Yoast | Webflow | Different stacks, similar capabilities |

## 5. AEO / GEO Signals

### FAQ schema presence
- **NFG product page:** FAQ schema present (4 questions: What is NFG / When did Bonterra acquire NFG / Security standards / Onboarding & migration) — good AEO coverage for brand-defense queries.
- **Blog posts:** Mixed. The "31+ top solutions" article has an FAQ H2 but confirmation of FAQ schema in source needs a deeper crawl — likely present given Yoast patterns. The major-gifts guide does **not** have FAQ schema (confirmed).
- **FAQ coverage gap:** No FAQ schema on pricing page, comparison-style content, or coaching pages.

### E-E-A-T signals
| Signal | NFG/Bonterra | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Visible author byline on blog posts | **Missing** (Person schema exists but no rendered byline on major-gifts, donor-management-software guides) | Weak |
| Author bio pages / expertise profiles | Not linked from blog footer | Missing |
| Credentials / certifications referenced | Generic "22+ years of nonprofit experience" — no specific authors cited | Weak |
| Original research / proprietary data | Yes — "90,000+ campaign dataset" referenced | Strong |
| Customer logos / case studies | 252 case studies indexed | Very strong |
| Awards / badges | G2, TrustRadius badges on product page | Strong |
| Trust signals (SOC 2, security) | Referenced in FAQ but not prominently displayed | Moderate |

### llms.txt / GEO readiness
- **bonterratech.com/llms.txt:** 404 (confirmed)
- **networkforgood.com/llms.txt:** 403 (pre-redirect)
- **No llms.txt file on Bonterra at all.** This is a clear GEO-readiness gap. DonorDock has an llms.txt (confirmed at donordock.com/llms.txt) and this is a net advantage for DonorDock in the AI-answer layer.

### Citation readiness for AI engines
- **Headline structure:** Bonterra uses clear H1/H2/H3 hierarchy — good for extraction.
- **Definitional paragraphs:** Strong. Articles open with a clear definition answer pattern (e.g., "Major gifts are essential to fundraising success...") — extractable.
- **Table / list content:** Extensive use of numbered lists and tables — high extractability.
- **Anchor text internal linking:** Heavy internal linking but most anchors are generic ("learn more," "read more"). Mixed for AI citation.
- **Date freshness:** Content is re-dated ("2026" in titles) but publication dates visible — AI engines can assess recency.

**Net AEO/GEO grade:** B-. Strong topical coverage and FAQ schema on product, but missing llms.txt, missing visible author E-E-A-T, missing product schema, missing FAQ schema on pricing and key guides.

## 6. Content Strategy Gaps NFG Exploits

These are terrain NFG/Bonterra currently owns where DonorDock is absent or underweight:

1. **"Best of" listicles at category level.** Bonterra ranks on "best nonprofit software," "best donor management software," "best nonprofit CRM" with 3,000–10,500 word pillar articles refreshed for 2026. DonorDock has `/articles/best-nonprofit-crm` (noted in search) but Bonterra's footprint is broader and more internally-linked.

2. **Alternatives / comparison content as category plays.** `/blog/givebutter-alternatives` is a clean programmatic page capturing "[competitor] alternatives" intent. Bonterra does not target "DonorDock alternatives" — but they could. This is a defensive content need for DonorDock.

3. **Peer-to-peer, events, and auctions.** NFG positions P2P as a core strength. DonorDock does not offer P2P and has less content on these topics, ceding the intent cluster.

4. **Topical authority on fundraising strategy (non-product content).** Major gifts, planned giving, matching gifts, year-end receipts — Bonterra has 2,500–4,000 word guides ranking. Purely educational, non-product intent.

5. **Training / education hub as SEO asset.** 185 training URLs indexed at `/training/[product]/[course]`. Bonterra Academy is both a customer-retention and SEO asset (long-tail course title queries). DonorDock Academy exists but URL inventory is smaller.

6. **Case study content volume.** 252 case-study URLs. These are high-converting bottom-funnel content and Bonterra has 6–10x DonorDock's inventory.

7. **"Buyer's guide" framing.** Multiple 5,000+ word buyer's guides with FAQ schema targeting commercial investigation intent ("what to look for in a nonprofit CRM," "how to choose a donor management platform").

## 7. Content Gaps NFG Leaves Open for DonorDock

Where NFG is weak, absent, or tonally wrong — DonorDock's opening:

1. **Pricing transparency content.** NFG pricing is gated behind demo, with reviews citing 4.75% transaction fees and non-cancellable contracts. DonorDock has an exposed $500/mo + 1% fee — this is a content moat. Build: `/guides/nonprofit-crm-pricing-transparency`, `/guides/hidden-fees-in-nonprofit-software`, a "real cost of Network for Good" explainer.

2. **Migration / "leaving Bonterra" intent.** G2/Trustpilot reviews cite support-failure and data-loss horror stories. There is zero "how to switch from Network for Good" content from neutral or favorable sources. Build: `/guides/how-to-switch-from-network-for-good`, `/guides/migrate-from-bonterra-to-donordock`, data-export walkthrough content.

3. **Author-bylined E-E-A-T content.** Bonterra blog posts have no visible author bylines. DonorDock can differentiate by publishing with visible experts (Matt Bitzegaio, Rob Burke, customer success leaders) with full author pages and `sameAs` links to LinkedIn/Twitter.

4. **Small-nonprofit-specific language.** Bonterra claims "small nonprofits" but the site reads enterprise-y (advocacy, CSR, grants). DonorDock can capture "1–5 person nonprofit," "executive director doing everything," "fundraiser wearing 5 hats" long-tail intent.

5. **Contract flexibility / no-long-term-contract positioning.** Every piece of content that mentions "no annual contract" or "month-to-month" is a direct anti-NFG wedge. Build a contract-flexibility page and a "what to ask before signing a 3-year nonprofit software contract" guide.

6. **Short-form AEO-friendly answer content.** NFG/Bonterra content is long-form pillar style. Short, direct-answer 800-1,200-word posts with crisp FAQ schema are currently under-served in this competitive niche and are precisely what AI answer engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Overviews) surface.

7. **Alternatives pages for *NFG/Bonterra* specifically.** DonorDock already has `/compare/network-for-good-vs-donordock`. Expand to `/alternatives/network-for-good`, `/alternatives/bonterra`, `/alternatives/bonterra-guided-fundraising` to capture all rebrand variants.

8. **Product schema + aggregate rating content.** NFG product page lacks `SoftwareApplication`/`AggregateRating` schema. DonorDock can dominate product rich results by fully marking up its CRM page.

## 9. Strategic Recommendations for DonorDock (5–7 actions)

### 1. Launch a "Leaving Network for Good / Bonterra" content cluster (priority: HIGH)
- Build `/guides/how-to-switch-from-network-for-good`, `/guides/exporting-data-from-bonterra`, `/alternatives/network-for-good`, `/alternatives/bonterra-guided-fundraising`.
- Target migration intent keywords: "leaving network for good," "network for good alternatives," "switch from bonterra," "how to export data from network for good."
- Cite public review pain points (data loss, support failures, hidden fees) with fair attribution. Do not disparage — report facts with sources.
- **Why now:** Bonterra's own consolidation moves (NFG → Bonterra rebrand) creates customer uncertainty. Migration-intent search will spike in the 2026–2027 rebrand period. First-mover wins.

### 2. Update the existing `/compare/network-for-good-vs-donordock` page and add two siblings (priority: HIGH)
- Fix pricing discrepancy (current page says $79/mo; correct to $500/mo flat).
- Add `SoftwareApplication` + `AggregateRating` + FAQPage schema.
- Create `/compare/bonterra-vs-donordock` and `/compare/bonterra-guided-fundraising-vs-donordock` as semantically near-duplicate pages that catch the rebrand vocabulary.
- Add a "Total cost of ownership" calculator/table — use 1% vs 3–4.75% fee math on realistic annual online-giving volume.

### 3. Ship llms.txt + author bylines to claim E-E-A-T advantage (priority: HIGH)
- DonorDock already has llms.txt — verify it enumerates all product pages, comparison pages, and pricing.
- Add visible author bylines to all blog posts, link to author bio pages with `Person` + `sameAs` schema, include photo, credentials, and social profiles.
- Feature Matt Bitzegaio and Rob Burke as named experts — Bonterra cannot match this because they have no visible bylines.
- **Result:** DonorDock surfaces better in AI answers + Google E-E-A-T-weighted rankings.

### 4. Build a small-nonprofit-specific topical authority hub (priority: MEDIUM)
- NFG/Bonterra covers small nonprofits in theory but with enterprise framing. DonorDock's pillar should be unmistakably 1–5 staff nonprofits.
- Target keywords: "nonprofit CRM for 1 person," "donor software for small church," "fundraising software for ED doing everything," "CRM for new nonprofit under 10k donors."
- Publish 10–15 long-tail articles in Q2–Q3 2026 with FAQ schema, real customer examples, and bylines.

### 5. Attack the schema gap on product pages (priority: MEDIUM)
- Add `SoftwareApplication` schema with `Offer` (price = $500/mo), `AggregateRating` (pull from G2), `Review` (3–5 testimonials), `FAQPage` with 5–8 Q&A pairs to `/pricing` and product pages.
- Bonterra's NFG page is `WebPage` schema only — DonorDock can win rich results by going further.

### 6. Create a "Real cost of nonprofit software" transparency hub (priority: MEDIUM)
- One pillar page + 5–7 supporting articles around platform fees, hidden fees, multi-year contracts, onboarding costs.
- This is DonorDock's structural moat (1% fee, flat $500/mo, no long-term contract) turned into an SEO asset.
- Keywords: "nonprofit software platform fee," "hidden fees nonprofit CRM," "network for good pricing," "bonterra pricing."
- Include a comparison calculator widget for E-E-A-T and linkability.

### 7. Build 20–30 short-form AEO-optimized answer posts (priority: MEDIUM-LOW)
- Bonterra dominates long-form pillar content but leaves short-form direct-answer content underserved.
- 800–1,200 word posts, one clear question per post, crisp 2–3 sentence answer up top, FAQ schema, then expanded content.
- Targets: "what is a donor CRM," "how much does nonprofit software cost," "what is a platform fee," "do I need a CRM for my nonprofit of 5 people," "is network for good expensive."
- Designed to be cited verbatim by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews.
