# DonorDock Objection Handling Playbook

This document covers the most common objections the sales team encounters, with suggested approaches for each. These aren't scripts — adapt them to the conversation and prospect.

---

## Pricing & Cost Objections

### "It's too expensive" / "We don't have the budget"

**What's really going on:** They may not yet see the ROI, or they're comparing to free/cheap tools without accounting for total cost of ownership.

**Approach:**
- Acknowledge budget is real: "Budget matters, especially for nonprofits — we get that."
- Reframe from cost to consolidation: "Most orgs we work with are paying for 3-5 separate tools — CRM, email, giving pages, texting, maybe a project management tool. DonorDock replaces all of those in one plan. When you add up what you're spending now across all those tools, DonorDock usually comes out even or cheaper."
- Anchor on value: "Everything is included — unlimited contacts, unlimited users, email, text, online giving, AI, automations. No feature gates, no surprise upgrades."
- Safety net: "And there's a 90-day money-back guarantee, so there's no risk in trying it."

**What not to say:** Never say DonorDock is "cheap" or a "budget option." We are affordable and high-value.

---

### "We can't justify a new expense right now"

**Approach:**
- Explore what they're currently spending: "What are you using today for CRM, email, and online giving? When you look at the combined cost of those tools plus the time your team spends switching between them, how does that compare?"
- Frame as investment in efficiency: "The teams we work with typically save 5-10 hours a week by consolidating into one platform. That's staff time you're already paying for."
- Offer timeline flexibility: "No contracts — you can start when the timing is right and there's no long-term commitment."

---

### "[Competitor] is cheaper"

**What's really going on:** At $500/mo, DonorDock's sticker price is higher than some competitors' base pricing. The key is total cost of ownership and what's actually included.

**Approach:**
- Acknowledge the price difference honestly: "You're right — our sticker price is higher than their base plan. Here's why that's misleading to compare directly."
- Break down what they'd actually pay: "Most competitors charge separately for CRM, email, online giving, text messaging, and volunteer management. When you add those modules together, plus per-contact fees as your database grows, the total often exceeds what DonorDock costs — and you still don't get AI or project management."
- For Bloomerang specifically: "Bloomerang's base CRM starts lower, but it's modular. Email, fundraising tools, volunteer management — each is a separate charge. Contacts are limited and pricing scales as you grow. Add it all up and you're often paying more for less."
- Anchor on what $500 gets: "With DonorDock, $500/mo gets you everything — CRM, email, text, online giving, AI, project management, automations, unlimited contacts, unlimited users. No surprises, no upgrades, no scaling penalties."
- If they genuinely can't afford $500/mo: Be honest about fit. "If $500/mo is outside your budget right now, that's okay — we might not be the right fit today. We're built for organizations that are ready to invest in a system their whole team will use every day."

## Switching & Migration Objections

### "Switching CRMs sounds painful"

**What's really going on:** Fear of data loss, downtime, learning curve, and the sheer work of migrating.

**Approach:**
- Validate the concern: "Switching CRMs is one of the scariest decisions a nonprofit makes. Your data is everything."
- Lead with the migration support: "We do a free, human-assisted data migration. A real person works with you to move your data — contacts, gifts, history, custom fields. You're not left to figure it out with a CSV template."
- Speed and ease: "DonorDock is rated #1 Easiest Setup on G2 — our implementation is 3.5x faster than the industry average. Most teams are up and running in days, not months."
- Reduce perceived risk: "And with the 90-day money-back guarantee, if it's not the right fit, you get a full refund."

---

### "We just signed a contract with [competitor]"

**Approach:**
- Respect the decision: "Makes sense — if you just made that commitment, we wouldn't want you to rush a change."
- Plant the seed: "When that contract comes up for renewal, we'd love to be part of the conversation. A lot of organizations come to us after a year with another platform when they realize they need more from their system."
- Stay helpful: "In the meantime, I can send you a quick comparison so you have it when the time comes. No pressure."

---

### "Our team is resistant to change"

**Approach:**
- Empathize: "That's one of the biggest challenges with any new system — getting the team on board."
- Use the adoption stat: "DonorDock has roughly 20% higher user adoption than competitors. The reason is the interface — it's genuinely intuitive. Your team members don't need a training course to use it."
- ActionBoard as the hook: "The thing that usually wins teams over is ActionBoard. They open it each morning and it tells them exactly what to do — who to follow up with, who to thank. It's like having a personal assistant. That's usually the 'aha' moment."

## Feature & Capability Objections

### "Does DonorDock do [specific thing]?"

**Approach:**
- If yes: Explain the feature in benefit terms, then confirm: "Yes — [benefit explanation]. That's [feature name]."
- If no: Be honest. "That's not something we offer today. Here's what we do have that might get you close..." Don't oversell.
- If unsure: "Let me check on that and get back to you with a clear answer." Never guess.

---

### "We need really robust reporting"

**Approach:**
- Acknowledge the need: "Reporting is critical — especially for board meetings and grant applications."
- Position the difference: "DonorDock's reporting is visual and drag-and-drop. You don't need formulas or coding to build custom reports. That's a big difference from legacy systems like DonorPerfect where reporting often requires formula-based queries."
- Be honest about limits: "If you need Salesforce-level custom report building with dozens of cross-object relationships, that's beyond what we're designed for. But for the reporting 95% of growing nonprofits need — giving summaries, donor retention, campaign performance, board-ready dashboards — we've got you covered."

---

### "We need peer-to-peer fundraising"

**Approach:**
- Be honest: "Peer-to-peer isn't our core strength. If that's your primary fundraising model, platforms like Givebutter or Classy might be a better starting point."
- Explore further: "That said — is peer-to-peer your main fundraising channel, or one of several? If it's supplementary, a lot of orgs use a P2P tool alongside DonorDock as their central CRM and pipe everything through Zapier."

---

### "We're mostly grant-funded"

**Approach:**
- Qualify honestly: "DonorDock is built primarily for organizations that fundraise from individual donors. If grants are 80%+ of your revenue and individual giving is a small slice, we might not be the best fit right now."
- Explore the mix: "But if you're looking to grow your individual giving program alongside grants — which a lot of orgs are — that's exactly where DonorDock shines. We help you build and steward that donor base."

## Competitive Objections

### "We're looking at Bloomerang too"

**Approach:**
- Acknowledge: "Bloomerang is a solid option — they do a good job with retention analytics."
- Differentiate on action vs. analytics: "The biggest difference is philosophy. Bloomerang focuses on showing you how you're doing — retention scores, analytics. DonorDock focuses on helping you take action — what to do today, who to follow up with, which donors need attention right now."
- Differentiate on platform: "Bloomerang has grown through acquisitions (Kindful, etc.), so the experience can feel stitched together across modules. DonorDock is one unified platform — everything was built to work together from the start."
- Differentiate on pricing: "Their pricing scales per module and per contact count. Ours is one plan, everything included, unlimited contacts."

---

### "We're looking at Network for Good"

**Approach:**
- Acknowledge: "Network for Good has been in the space for a long time."
- Key differences: "A few things to compare: NfG requires a 36-month contract — we don't do contracts. They charge 3% on every transaction — with DonorDock, processing can be donor-funded so your org pays zero. And they don't include email marketing, text messaging, or AI — you'd need separate tools for those. When you factor in transaction fees and the tools you'd need to add on, the total cost picture looks different than the sticker price suggests."

---

### "We're looking at DonorPerfect"

**Approach:**
- Acknowledge: "DonorPerfect has deep roots in the nonprofit space."
- Key difference — usability: "The main thing we hear from orgs switching from DonorPerfect is that their team wasn't actually using it. The interface is complex, reporting requires formula-based coding, and adoption tends to be low. DonorDock is rated 9.6 for Ease of Use on G2 (vs. DonorPerfect's 8.4). If getting your whole team to actually use the CRM matters, that gap is significant."
- Pricing: "Similar price point, but DonorPerfect limits contacts and charges for add-ons. DonorDock is everything included."

---

### "Why haven't I heard of DonorDock?"

**Approach:**
- Own it: "We're growing but we're not as well-known as some of the bigger names yet. That's fair."
- Let the numbers speak: "What we do have is 7,200+ users, 4.8 stars on G2 and Capterra, and #1 rankings for Easiest Setup, Best Support, and Easiest to Use. We also just hit G2's Top 50 and Momentum Leader lists."
- Frame the upside: "Being newer also means you get a modern platform built with today's tech — AI, smart automations, a unified experience — not a legacy system with 20 years of technical debt."

## Fit & Qualification Objections

### "We're too small for a CRM"

**Approach:**
- Explore where they are: "How many donors are you managing right now, and how are you tracking them?"
- If they're genuinely early-stage (under $500K, 1-2 staff, few hundred donors): "Honestly, if you're tracking a few hundred donors and it's just one person managing everything, you might not need a CRM yet. A spreadsheet or free tool could work for now. When you start dropping follow-ups or your board starts asking for reports you can't pull easily — that's when to revisit."
- If they're further along than they think: "A lot of orgs say 'we're small' but they're managing 2,000+ donors across spreadsheets, Gmail, and a giving platform. If that sounds like you, you're actually past the point where manual tracking works well."

---

### "We already use spreadsheets and they work fine"

**Approach:**
- Don't attack spreadsheets: "Spreadsheets are great — until they're not. They work well for tracking a few hundred donors. Where they break down is when multiple people need access, when you need to track communication history, or when the board asks 'how's our donor retention?'"
- Plant the question: "Do you ever miss a follow-up because it wasn't in the spreadsheet? Has a donor ever gotten the wrong communication? When you need to pull a report for the board, how long does that take?"
- Bridge: "DonorDock is for the moment when spreadsheets start creating more work than they save."
