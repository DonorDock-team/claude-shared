The path to the walled garden

Start safe. Build toward the network.

Every near-term move ships value on its own and quietly builds the data infrastructure the graph will need.

Horizon 1 · now · low risk
  • Benchmarks in-product: "how am I doing vs. peers like me," a reason to stay.
  • "State of Donor Stewardship" report: a benchmark built from our aggregated behavioral data, retention, lapse, and engagement, that FEP and the big studies do not cover. Authority, PR, and lead-gen.
  • Aggregate-trained AI in Otto: lapse risk, next best action, suggested ask.
Horizon 2 · medium
  • Intelligence tier: package benchmarks, scoring, and enrichment as paid expansion.
  • Opt-in cross-org signals: for each org that opts in, surface prospect and capacity insights drawn from the wider network. The first live pilots of the graph.
  • Behavior-based enrichment that recaptures wealth-screening spend.
Horizon 3 · the walled garden
  • Consent-based donor graph: prospect intelligence and capacity scoring drawn from the whole network, offered as a premium capability.
  • Network data services: shared suppression, address, and deceased flags that keep every org's data clean.
  • Win-back signals: alert orgs when a lapsed donor gives to a peer, so they can re-engage.
The benchmark report is the visible first move: low risk, high authority, and it seeds the graph
A decision to surface

Own the layer, don't feed it.

Today the DonorDock + Avid AI integration is a one-way export of our donor data into a partner's "fundraising operating system." That is worth examining.

The risk

If our crown-jewel data becomes the fuel for someone else's intelligence layer, we risk being relegated to the system of record while the partner owns the relationship, the insight, and the upsell.

The recommendation

Be the system of record and the intelligence layer. Let partners integrate with DonorDock while we own the data and the donor relationship. Partnerships sit at the edges; the data layer stays ours.

Keep good partnerships. Keep the data layer ours.
The foundation

Trust is the moat. Protect it first.

Donor data is sacred, and our brand is built on warmth and trust. The data strategy only works if it strengthens that trust rather than spending it.

Never sell donor data

Full stop. The moment we monetize donors instead of serving them, the brand and the moat both break.

Opt-in and aggregated

Cross-org features are consent-based and aggregated by default. This is the AFP Donor Bill of Rights, not a constraint we invented.

Collective intelligence

Frame it as a member benefit for the sector, never as surveillance. Trust architecture ships before the graph does.

AFP Donor Bill of Rights (1993) and eDonor Bill of Rights (2019): donors may opt out of shared lists and must be told what is collected and how it is used.

Recommendation & next steps

Five decisions for leadership.

1
Treat data as strategic infrastructure

Fund the pipeline and team that turn multi-tenant data into benchmarks, scores, and the graph.

2
Ship the benchmark report

The visible, low-risk first move, and what FEP will not do: gate it. Authority, leads, and the seed of the data layer.

3
Productize the intelligence layer

Package benchmarks, scoring, and enrichment into a paid tier. The first expansion revenue, and the bridge to the graph.

4
Set the trust architecture now

Consent, privacy, and terms designed before any cross-org feature ships. Non-negotiable.

5
Revisit the Avid AI relationship

Own the intelligence layer. Reposition partners to build on us, not to absorb our data.

What it requires: data engineering, a privacy and legal partner, and product focus. Modest investment against a category-defining payoff.
